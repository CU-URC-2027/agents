# Research standards

## Evidence ledger

Record each material claim in this shape while researching:

| Claim | Source | Source type | Date | What it supports | Caveat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Keep the claim narrower than the source when necessary. A source that reports a finding in one population does not automatically establish the same result everywhere.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer the closest available source to the claim:

1. Original study, official standard, primary dataset, or first-party technical documentation.
2. Systematic review, research synthesis, or an independent replication.
3. Reputable institutional or professional reporting that links to its basis.
4. Commentary and search snippets only for discovery, never as sole support for a material claim.

Authority does not eliminate the need to check a source's scope, date, methods, and conflicts of interest.

## Claim rules

- Cite direct factual, numerical, causal, or disputed claims beside the claim.
- Use two independent sources when feasible for a decision that is costly, safety-critical, contentious, or based on a single surprising finding.
- Name uncertainty: small sample, observational design, a dated source, unreviewed preprint, incomplete access, or inconsistent results.
- Say `I could not verify this` instead of using a weak source to fill a gap.
- Separate a recommendation from the evidence it is based on.
